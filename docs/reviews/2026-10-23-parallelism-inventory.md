# Parallelism in the framework: what is built, accepted or dropped, and what a central session would add (2026-10-23, report only)

The owner asked what parallelism the framework already has, has accepted or has dropped, as the basis for deciding whether a "central session" (one coordinator that knows the order of the work, runs several build sessions in parallel, keeps them from clashing and relays between them and the owner) should become a pipeline feature for projects. This page is the study: an inventory in two scopes, a gap analysis and options with a recommendation. It changes no code and takes no decision; it adds no ROADMAP row and takes no number.

## 2. Gap analysis: what a pipeline-level central coordinator would need

The owner's description names five jobs: knowing the order of the work, running several build sessions in parallel, preventing their clashes, relaying between them and the owner, and (implicit in the waves) the merge order. Each is taken against section 1, with where its parts would live.

|---|---|---|---|

## 3. Options

Each option is judged against the owner's weights (`playbook-vs-framework.md:111`: owner attention 3, autonomy 3, auditability 2, fidelity 2, safety 1, cost 0) and names the decisions it touches.

### Option A. No pipeline feature: the central session stays a practice of this repository (choice 262 as #352 proposes it)
- **What it is.** #352 merges as written: `central-session.md` and `waves.md` govern this repository's waves; projects keep A1 to A22, with R25 part 3, R52, R9 part 1 and R20 at their places in the Order column, and R50's trigger asks about step 33.
- **Cost.** None beyond #352. The known costs of the practice stay (section 1(b)'s last paragraph): a renumber per wave, the owner's effort setting and "ready" click per lane, a plan that nothing checks.
- **Against the weights.** Owner attention: unchanged for projects; for this repository the owner talks to one session (the practice's point). Autonomy: unchanged. Auditability: the waves and the lane prompts are in the repository, but the relay and the hourly check leave no record (B11, B12). Fidelity: the article's parallelism is worktrees for file-disjoint tasks of one engineer (p.25); the practice is outside the article, as this repository's development is.
- **Decisions touched.** None.

### Option B. The deterministic parts in the pipeline, no coordinator: "changes in flight, ordered and warned"
- **What it is.** One ROADMAP candidate with a proposal: (1) a dependency line in the intent, kept in `status.yaml`, and a check at the (e) offer that holds a PR until its dependency is merged; (2) a file-overlap warning in the design run, from plan.md's "Files that change" against the open build branches, printed on the PR and in the digest, never a park; (3) R51's two follow-ups (check 8 and check 11's record). R25 part 3, R9 part 1 and R20 stay where they are and give the ceiling and the view.
- **Cost.** A proposal, then one plugin change of R51's size or smaller; a template change for `status.yaml` and `changes/README.md`. Judged at R50's trigger with R52, or placed by the owner after the trial, so that the trial measures the pipeline as it is.
- **Against the weights.** Owner attention: down, since the owner stops discovering a dependency or an overlap at a park. Autonomy: up by the same amount. Auditability: up, since the dependency and the warning are in the change's files. Fidelity: the overlap warning is step 33's "proven file-disjoint" read (p.25) applied across changes instead of inside one; the dependency line is new but deterministic. Safety: unchanged, since nothing merges that did not before.
- **Decisions touched.** None reopened: decision 11 is untouched by a warning; decision 36 is untouched if the hold is at the (e) offer, before the owner's review; R9, R20 and R51 are extended, not changed. The proposal would say so row by row, as `concurrent-changes.md`'s "Decisions it touches" does.

### Option C. A central session as a pipeline feature for projects
- **What it is.** What the owner described, for a project: a long-lived coordinator session that reads a committed plan (a `waves.md` in `changes/`), starts a change's sessions, pairs changes by their plans, allocates nothing (ids are reserved already), relays the owner's answers between PRs, reports hourly and names the merge order.
- **Cost.** High, and most of it outside the plugin: `create_session`, `send_message`, `get_session` and the scheduled task are Claude Code Remote features, not GitHub Actions; a project that runs the pipeline only in CI (the operating model's end state, `docs/OPERATING_MODEL.md`) has no coordinator to host. The state of the coordinator (its trigger id, the lanes' session ids) lives outside the repository until an end file names it (B11, B12). The first central session "grew long" coordinating two waves (`docs/progress/2026-10-23-ccr-b2d02bf7-9ywgjn-central.md`, "Why"), so one coordinator per project is one more context to hand over.
- **Against the weights.** Owner attention: it is the practice's reason, and for a project it is already served per change by the review request (decision 31) and the digest (R20); a coordinator adds one voice between the owner and the PRs. Autonomy: not up, since the coordinator cannot merge, un-park or start a change without reopening the decisions below. Auditability: down unless every relay is also written to the receiving PR. Fidelity: the article has no coordinator; its parallelism is 2 to 3 streams one person can review (p.25), which decision 17 already settles and R50 revisits. Safety: a relay that carries the owner's words as acts is the exposure R28's rule 8 closes for the manager.
- **Decisions touched.** 17 (parallel streams "probably never for one person", revisited only at R50's trigger; a coordinator that multiplies streams reopens it before the trigger); 11 and 31 (a coordinator that reports hourly and relays is a second channel, against "nobody is notified" and "nothing else notifies"); 24 (the owner's un-park verbs are the owner's labels; a coordinator that relays an answer as a label acts in the owner's name, which `is_owner_actor` refuses, `24.md:14`); 4 and 36 (it must never merge; today's practice keeps the merges the owner's, `central-session.md:61`); 5 and 33 (which identity a coordinator's writes use); R28 (a second agent that "holds the bigger picture", beside the manager that is accepted to do so inside a gate).

### Option D. Option B for projects, and the practice hardened for this repository
- **What it is.** Option B as the project-side candidate; #352 merges for this repository; and the practice's mechanical weaknesses go into `tasks.py` with no plugin version: a task that compares the paths named by two proposals (B9), `next-free` printing the clash it already sees as a warning on `verify` when the branch's own number is taken elsewhere (B3), and `HANDOFF.md`'s "newest file" rule corrected to the lineage in the headers (B1; a page the owner changes in a PR of its own).
- **Cost.** Option B's plus three small documentation-and-`tasks.py` PRs in the small lane.
- **Against the weights.** As option B for projects; for this repository, fewer false claims by the coordinator (B9) and no misread of the newest file (B1), at the cost of nothing the owner does.
- **Decisions touched.** As option B; the `HANDOFF.md` correction touches R9 part 2's amended text (`state-index.md:61`, "the listing sorted by name is the index"), which is a choice of the owner's and needs the owner's word.

### Recommendation
**Option D, with option B placed after the trial.** The reasons, each from section 1 or 2:
1. A project's unit of parallel work is the change, and the pipeline already runs changes in parallel safely (R51 built; A2 to A8). The jobs a coordinator does for this repository that a project lacks are two deterministic ones, ordering and overlap, which belong in the gate and the digest, not in a session.
2. The jobs that only a session can do, starting work and relaying the owner's words, are the jobs the settled decisions keep with the owner (11, 24, 31, 36) and the ones R28's manager is allowed only through the owner's own signal. Making them a pipeline feature reopens five decisions for a gain the weights do not reward: owner attention is already served per PR, and auditability falls.
3. The article's own ceiling (p.25, decision 17) is the owner's review, and R50 already asks the right question at 20 changes. The trial should measure the pipeline without a coordinator first, as the owner chose for R28 (choice 238).
4. This repository's practice has shown its costs in one day (two number clashes, two false claims, a renumber per wave); they are cheap to close in `tasks.py` and the pages, and closing them there is where the evidence is.

### What the owner must decide
1. **Scope of choice 262.** Whether the central session is a practice of this repository only (merge #352 as written) or also a candidate for projects. A candidate needs a proposal under `docs/proposals/` and a ROADMAP row the owner places; this page is not one.
2. **Option B's place.** Whether "changes in flight, ordered and warned" is written as a proposal now and placed after the trial, or waits for R50's trigger with R52. Either way it takes the next free candidate number from `python tasks.py next-free` when it is written, not here.
3. **The number clash in this repository.** Whether "one lane renumbers after the merge" (B10) stays the rule, or a reservation closes it, which reopens choices 184 and 252 and needs the owner's word before anyone builds it.
4. **The newest-file rule.** Whether `HANDOFF.md`'s "sorted by name is the index" becomes "the lineage in the headers is the index" (B1). It is a change to a stable page, in a PR of its own.
5. **R51's two follow-ups** (check 8, check 11's record): whether they are wanted, as the R51 session file left them (its next step 3).
