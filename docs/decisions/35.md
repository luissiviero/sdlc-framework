## 35. A risk-list hit is a line in the close-look banner (amends 11, 21 and 24)

- Today (decisions 11, 21 and 24): a risk-list hit is a hard park. Decision 11 counts it among the parks, decision 21 puts it on the list that never goes to the review panel and still parks, and decision 24 makes `sdlc:accept-risk` the label that lifts it: a fix round records the item and re-runs the phase. Accepting a risk costs the owner two acts and a session, the label and then the merge the owner would give anyway. The check matches each item's words in the committed diff's file paths and in spec.md's "Flagged concerns" (`plugin/gate/checks.py` `risk_hits`), so most real paths of the four default items pass, while a change whose slug carries an item's word hits (the proposal, "What the framework does today").
- **Accepted:**
  1. **The banner replaces the park.** A risk-list hit no longer parks. It is a line of the "Needs your close look" banner on the PR the owner merges anyway: a hit found at (b) on the design PR, a hit first found at (c), (d) or (e) on the build PR at (e). Under the Full profile it also shows in the banner of the PR that waits for `sdlc:c-approved` or `sdlc:d-approved`, where the owner can stop it with "Request changes"; only the (e) merge accepts it (R12's third point, settled at test-gate, T12).
  2. **The merge is the acceptance, recorded.** The runner records which items were accepted, by whom, when, and that the banner showed them, in `status.yaml` beside the plan-acceptance line (R11's approvals list). The actor is the merged pull request's merge actor. *[2026-10-20, choice 242: once R22 is built, the act that accepts is the owner's approving review (decision 36), recorded on R22's (e) approval line; the App that then merges is a `[bot]` and never accepts a risk]*
  3. **The reviewer briefs, never accepts.** At each gate where an item hits, that gate's reviewer writes a short brief per item (what the change does to it, what could break, how to undo it), *[2026-10-25, R45's second rename PR (decision 39, rows 62 and N17; plugin 1.0.44): each item's field "Word" is "Verdict" and its value `continue | park`; a brief with "Word" or `escalate` is read for one release]* and the banner quotes it: the adversarial reviewer at (b) to (d), the REVIEW.md pass at (e), the Security & policy role once R28 builds it. No extra run: these reviewers already run at those gates. The reviewer's escalation on a risk item still parks for the owner, and risk items stay off the review panel.
  4. **Each risk item lists its paths.** An item gains the globs it covers. `/sdlc-init` proposes them from the project's layout and the owner confirms the list once, in the change-0000 PR. The check matches the globs on the committed diff and, as today, the item's words in "Flagged concerns".
  5. **The label retires.** `sdlc:accept-risk` is no longer an un-park verb; the by-hand `state/cli.py accept-risk` stays.
  6. **The ratchet findings.** A ratchet finding that R15 records as a risk-list hit is a banner line the owner's merge accepts, as every hit is, and a drop the approved plan declares shows as a banner line too.
  7. **The documents follow.** The sentences that say a hit parks, the `data-conventions` skill's "parks at its next gate" among them, are corrected in the change that builds this.
- Rejected: keeping today's label (two acts and a fix session to accept what the owner merges anyway); option B of build-risk (the merge accepts at (b) only, and a hit first found later still parks for the label); a hook that blocks risk paths during (c) unless the item was accepted at (b) (closer to the article, but in an unattended run it only moves the park earlier and leaves the work half done); an agent that accepts a risk for the owner (it runs on the session's own model and shares its blind spots; a brief, never an approval).

**Where the list stops:** secret-shaped paths (R61 part 5: `*.pem`, `*.key`, `credentials.json` and the like, which `/sdlc-init` proposes as `protected_paths` and `Read` deny rules) are files no session reads or writes, not risks the owner accepts by a merge, so they are never risk-list items and a risk item's glob never protects or opens a path.

**Why:** The article puts risky edits behind a person: "hooks can block edits to migrations and infra without a change ticket during Stage 3: Build", and "a hook can also ask, pausing the action until a specific person approves" (p.35); "the gate also defines what counts as approval, whether that's an approved change ticket or the release manager's sign-off" (p.37); "Humans remain accountable for every decision that requires judgment" (p.6). Here the change ticket is the owner's informed merge: the banner shows the item and the reviewer's brief on the PR the owner reads anyway, and the record keeps who accepted it and when. The park bought nothing the merge does not give: the owner accepted by a label what the owner then merged.

**What it gives up:** a risky build the design did not foresee is no longer stopped at (c) or (d); it runs on to (e) in the CI sandbox. Nothing is released before the owner's (e) merge, and on a declared production the release still waits for `sdlc:release-approved`; without a declared production the (e) merge releases, as for any change, with the risk accepted in the same act. Each project confirms its globs once at setup.
